Yes—there are real companies that examine a business’s payroll, accounting records, projects, locations, hiring, and investments to identify overlooked tax credits and help claim them. The category is usually called tax credits and incentives advisory, with newer software platforms automating parts of the discovery process. [anchin](https://www.anchin.com/services/credits-incentives/)

For U.S. businesses, the key **distinction** is between broad incentive discovery and deep analysis of one credit, especially the R&D credit. Some services search many programs; others connect directly to internal systems and build evidence for a specific claim. [incentify](https://www.incentify.com/explore)

## Real companies and how they work

### 1. MainStreet — software-assisted discovery and claiming for small businesses

MainStreet is one of the closest matches to “learn about my business and find credits I missed.”

- What it examines: A qualification questionnaire about your company, team, and work, plus payroll information connected through its platform.
- How it works: You answer eligibility questions, connect payroll, and provide the information needed to calculate qualifying expenses. MainStreet calculates the credits and prepares documentation for your CPA.
- What it delivers: Its current website emphasizes federal and state R&D credits, other small-business credits, and IRS-ready Form 6765 documentation.
- Important distinction: Its payroll integration partner describes broader discovery across hundreds of incentive programs, but its current public workflow is most explicit about R&D. Ask which non-R&D programs are actually included in your engagement. [tryfinch](https://www.tryfinch.com/customer-stories/mainstreet-and-finch-a-match-made-in-tax-credit-heaven)

This is a relevant starting point for an SMB owner seeking a guided service rather than a research database.

### 2. Neo.Tax — reads internal engineering and financial data to identify R&D credits

Neo.Tax is the closest technical match to “connect to company internals and determine what qualifies.”

- What it examines: Jira tickets, GitHub activity, payroll, general-ledger data, organizational structure, and other project documentation.
- How it works: Its AI analyzes engineering activity and connects projects to employee effort and expenses. Rather than relying primarily on retrospective employee surveys, it uses existing operational records to identify and document potentially qualifying work.
- What it delivers: R&D tax credit studies and supporting documentation. It also offers software-capitalization and R&D-capitalization analysis, which are related accounting/tax services—not additional tax credits.
- Important distinction: This is deep R&D analysis, not a general search for every hiring, property, energy, or local economic-development incentive. [tax.thomsonreuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/en/corporation-solutions/c/neotax)

Thomson Reuters also offers an ONESOURCE integration with Neo.Tax covering payroll, project-management, accounting, and ERP data, with a path into Form 6765 preparation. [tax.thomsonreuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/en/corporation-solutions/c/neotax)

### 3. Incentify — broad discovery across locations and incentive programs

Incentify is closer to an incentive search-and-management platform than an outsourced tax-preparation service.

- What it examines: Business locations, industry, business activities, and other eligibility information.
- How it works: Users enter an address, upload multiple locations, or complete an AI-guided review. The platform matches the business against its advertised database of more than 1,200 federal, state, and zone-based programs.
- What it delivers: Potential incentives, eligibility reviews, estimated values, filing requirements, and tools for tracking opportunities. Its management product also tracks existing incentives, compliance obligations, and expected payments.
- Important distinction: Finding a potential match is not the same as substantiating a claim or filing a return. Its discovery product is explicitly marketed to CPAs and advisers as well as businesses. [incentify](https://www.incentify.com/explore)

This is particularly relevant when the question is “What programs exist for our locations and operations?” rather than only “Does our software development qualify for R&D?”

### 4. Anchin — human-led, broad review of business operations and planned investments

Anchin’s Tax Credits and Incentives Group offers the traditional advisory version of what you described.

- What it examines: R&D activity, hiring, relocation, expansion, new facilities, and other business investments.
- How it works: Specialists analyze the company’s circumstances and identify applicable federal, state, and local programs, then help navigate the application and qualification process.
- What it delivers: Assistance obtaining credits and incentives across multiple categories—not just R&D.
- Concrete examples: Anchin reports helping a Long Island distributor access a package involving IDA, Excelsior Jobs, and energy-cost programs, and helping a Brooklyn food manufacturer obtain $2.3 million in credits over two years, half of which were refundable. Those are firm-reported client outcomes, not expected results for another business. [anchin](https://www.anchin.com/services/credits-incentives/)

Given your New York context, Anchin is especially worth examining for the combination of federal incentives and New York-specific opportunities.

### 5. Leyton — technical consultants investigate the work, tax consultants calculate the credit

Leyton’s U.S. R&D service uses a structured consulting process.

- What it examines: Financial records, development projects, technical activities, and associated research expenses.
- How it works: An initial eligibility review is followed by financial-data collection. Technical consultants evaluate projects against the R&D qualification tests, while tax consultants verify qualifying expenditures and calculate the credit.
- What it delivers: A detailed technical report, calculations, and supporting documentation for the claim.
- Timing: Leyton says a study typically takes one to two months after it receives the necessary financial and technical records, depending on complexity. [leyton](https://leyton.com/us/rd-tax-credit/)

This is a more hands-on alternative to connecting software and expecting automated discovery.

### 6. Corporate Tax Advisors — records-based review alongside your existing CPA

Corporate Tax Advisors, or CTA, explicitly describes a workflow for identifying and documenting potentially missed credits.

- What it examines: Prior-year tax returns, financial statements, payroll, project descriptions, and recent or planned investments.
- How it works: Discovery and screening → data collection → technical and financial analysis → calculations and documentation → coordination with your CPA → filing or amended-return work → follow-up support.
- What it delivers: Specialty credit studies and documentation coordinated with the business’s accountant.
- Commercial model: CTA states that its initial evaluation is free and that it performs studies on a contingency basis, meaning no fee unless credits are identified. Confirm the contract’s exact fee trigger: identifying a credit is not necessarily the same as receiving a refund. [corporatetaxadvisors](https://corporatetaxadvisors.com/tax-credits-for-businesses-a-practical-guide-for-small-business-owners/)

## How “finding credits” turns into a refund—or doesn’t

A credible service needs to do more than tell you that an activity sounds eligible:

1. Identify the activity or investment that may qualify.
2. Verify the program’s eligibility rules and deadlines.
3. Tie qualifying activity to documented expenses.
4. Calculate the benefit and prepare supporting records.
5. Coordinate the application, tax return, or amended return.
6. Address questions from the relevant tax authority. CTA’s published process follows this general sequence. [corporatetaxadvisors](https://corporatetaxadvisors.com/tax-credits-for-businesses-a-practical-guide-for-small-business-owners/)

The refund wording matters:

- A missed credit can sometimes support a refund claim on an amended return. The IRS specifically provides requirements for amended-return R&D refund claims, including identification of the relevant business components, research activities, and qualifying expenses. [irs](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/corporations/research-credit-claims-section-41-on-amended-returns-frequently-asked-questions)
- A credit may instead reduce tax owed or become a carryforward rather than produce an immediate payment. The IRS’s general-business-credit framework includes both current-year credits and carryforwards. [irs](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/business-tax-credits)
- Qualifying small businesses can elect to apply up to $500,000 of research credits against certain employer payroll taxes. That is a payroll-tax offset, not an unrestricted $500,000 refund, and the election generally must be made on a timely filed original income-tax return, including extensions. [irs](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/research-credit-against-payroll-tax-for-small-businesses)

## What to ask before signing

For this specific kind of service, I would request a written scope that answers:

- Which credits do you actually evaluate beyond R&D?
- Which internal systems do you access, and can we supply exports instead?
- Do you only identify opportunities, or also calculate, document, and help file them?
- Is your fee based on estimated credits, credits claimed, or cash actually received?
- Who handles an examination, and is that support included?
- Will our existing CPA review the position before anything is filed?

The most useful dividing line is this: Neo.Tax investigates internal work in depth; Incentify searches the incentive landscape broadly; MainStreet packages an SMB-oriented workflow; and firms such as Anchin, Leyton, and CTA provide people to investigate, substantiate, and help execute the claims. [anchin](https://www.anchin.com/services/credits-incentives/)
